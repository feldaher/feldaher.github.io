## Jelly Cells

An interactive soft-body simulation of five cells: a budding yeast, a red blood cell, a fibroblast, a microglial cell and a neuron. Each one behaves like a block of jelly. You can grab it, pull it, twist it and cut it with a knife to see its organelles in cross-section.

**[Open Jelly Cells](/jelly-cells/)** (needs a browser with WebGPU: recent Chrome, Edge or Safari)

### What you can do

- **Grab and pull** any part of the cell, and set how firm and how damped it is.
- **Cut** it with the knife. A readout tells you which organelles the cut went through and how big they are.
- **Move through stages** with a slider: the yeast cell cycle, microglial activation, a neuron's response to axon injury, red blood cell shape changes, and a fibroblast's wound response.
- **Click a label** to learn the real size and role of each structure, with a link to Wikipedia.
- **Switch to the Deformation view** to see where the cell is stretched or squeezed.

### The science

The cells are built from simple shapes, drawn at their real sizes, with bio-plausible anatomy. The mechanics are illustrative: each cell moves as if it were a few centimetres of gelatin, because at true size a cell is far too small and too viscous to visibly wobble.
